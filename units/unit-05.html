Why these readings

  • Deut 18:15–22The test for a prophet: if it doesn't happen, the LORD didn't say it. Every fulfillment claim in this unit has to pass it.
  • Isa 52:13–53:12The suffering servant. The NT applies it to Jesus by name (Acts 8:32–35), so you can see the fulfillment argument in the text instead of guessing at it.
  • Ps 22Jesus quotes its first line from the cross. Read the whole psalm and notice how much of the crucifixion scene is already there.
  • Mic 5:2Bethlehem as the ruler's birthplace. Matthew quotes it (Matt 2:5–6), which makes it one of the explicit fulfillment texts for your paper.
  • Dan 9:24–27A prophecy with a timeline. It's harder and more disputed than the others. It's here so you practice separating what the text says from what interpreters build on it.
  • Luke 24:13–49On the Emmaus road Jesus explains himself from "Moses and all the Prophets." This is Jesus making the prophecy argument himself.
  • Acts 8:26–40Prophecy used in a real conversation. Philip starts with the official's question, begins from Isa 53, and tells him about Jesus (8:35).

Word study

Say each word aloud and stress the bold syllable. Greek follows the classroom (Erasmian) pronunciation most seminaries teach; Hebrew follows standard academic pronunciation. Other accepted systems exist (e.g., modern Greek, reconstructed Koine), so you may hear these said differently. The author's context decides the meaning.

Questions

  1. What is the Deut 18 test?
  2. What does Luke actually tell us about how Jesus "opened the Scriptures" (Luke 24:27, 44–47), and what does he leave unsaid?
  3. How do direct prediction, typology, and promise–fulfillment differ?
  4. Why did Philip start with Isa 53?

Write

PostureAsk of every text

How should these texts shape the apologist's tone and conduct?

Paper1–2 pages

Trace the Old Testament passages explicitly quoted or clearly invoked in Acts 8:32–35, Matthew 2:5–6, and the passion accounts' use of Psalm 22. Classify each use as direct prediction, typology, or broader promise–fulfillment, explaining any overlap. Then use Luke 24:44–47 to propose what Jesus may have covered, clearly labeling that portion as inference.
